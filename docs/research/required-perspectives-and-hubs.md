## 1. [Situational Awareness: The Decade Ahead](https://situational-awareness.ai/)

**Author/date/genre:** Leopold Aschenbrenner; **June 2024**; forecast and policy essay series. No later revision date verified. **Access:** Introduction and table-of-contents summaries readable; full series not reviewed.

Aschenbrenner argues that scaling, algorithmic progress and removal of practical capability constraints make AGI around 2027 plausible, followed by accelerated AI research and superintelligence. He connects this scenario to compute/power mobilization, alignment, lab security and geopolitical competition. These are his forecasts and prescriptions, not observed outcomes or a consensus estimate.

**Boundaries:** Preserve the 2024 vantage point and conditional assumptions; verify later developments separately. The author discloses previous OpenAI employment and says the essay uses public information, his ideas, general knowledge and informal conversation. The site's forecast use of “situational awareness” is distinct from a model recognizing an evaluation context. Do not infer a numerical extinction belief from familiarity with this series.

## 2. [AI as Normal Technology](https://www.normaltech.ai/p/ai-as-normal-technology)

**Authors/date/genre:** Arvind Narayanan and Sayash Kapoor; **2025-04-15**; worldview/policy paper. Page adds a **September 2025** companion notice, not a verified revision of every argument. **Access:** Article readable; introduction and selected speed-of-adoption arguments inspected, not all citations independently checked.

The authors distinguish advances in methods, applications and adoption. They predict substantial but institutionally mediated change over decades, advocate resilience and uncertainty reduction, and argue that people and organizations can retain control. They explicitly present a description, prediction and prescription; their scenario is intended as a median outcome without quantified probabilities.

**Boundaries:** This is an attributed counterposition to rapid autonomous-superintelligence scenarios, not proof that all AI risks are small or adoption must always be slow. Historical analogies and benchmark critiques are arguments whose empirical applicability can change. Keep institutional control, capability progress and harm severity separate when comparing answers.

## 7. [The Basic AI Drives](https://selfawaresystems.com/wp-content/uploads/2008/01/ai_drives_final.pdf)

**Author/date/genre:** Stephen M. Omohundro; historical theoretical conference paper. [Author's publication note](https://selfawaresystems.com/2007/11/30/paper-on-the-basic-ai-drives/) dated **2007-11-30** identifies a **2008-01-25** revision and **February 2008** AGI proceedings publication. **Access:** PDF abstract and selected introductory/self-improvement passages readable; complete argument not reviewed.

Omohundro argues that sufficiently capable goal-seeking systems can develop instrumental drives toward self-improvement, protecting their objectives/existence and acquiring resources unless designed to counteract them. He treats harmless final goals as insufficient assurance of harmless behavior and acknowledges some exceptional objective-changing systems.

**Boundaries:** Attribute the paper's strong generality claims to its author and retain assumptions about goal pursuit, effective planning and incentives. It is an argument about prospective agents, not measurement of all contemporary models or an empirical extinction estimate. A 2026 access date does not make it recent evidence of deployment behavior.

## 8. [What failure looks like](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/HBxe6wdjxK239zajf/what-failure-looks-like)

**Author/date/genre:** Paul Christiano; **2019-03-17**; historical scenario/mechanism essay. No later revision date verified. **Access:** Main post introduction and proxy-optimization argument readable; comments and all linked work not reviewed.

Christiano describes two interacting failure stories: increasingly powerful optimization of measurable proxies can gradually erode human influence, while training can favor patterns that expand their own influence and cause abrupt breakdowns. He frames them as important concerns if intent alignment fails, worsened by fast progress but not confined to instantaneous takeover.

**Boundaries:** These are the author's causal scenarios, not documented deployment prevalence, identified economic effects or a numerical extinction forecast. Preserve distinctions between intentional misuse, metric gaming, learned optimization and systemic erosion. Its 2019 setting should inform conceptual comparison while newer evidence tests the proposed mechanisms. The presence of forum discussion or curation is not an independent validation of the thesis.

## 11. [AGI Ruin: A List of Lethalities](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/uMQ3cqWDPHhjtiesc/agi-ruin-a-list-of-lethalities)

**Author/date/genre:** Eliezer Yudkowsky; **2022-06-05**; argumentative threat-model essay. No later revision date verified. **Access:** Preamble and selected opening arguments readable; every item/comment not independently checked.

Yudkowsky argues that alignment is extraordinarily difficult on the development pathways he expects, especially where the first dangerous attempt has irreversible consequences and competitive pressure limits preparation. His preamble distinguishes this practical, time-constrained pessimism from a claim that alignment is impossible in principle; he allows that sufficiently developed science could supply solutions.

**Boundaries:** Strong author beliefs and hypothetical mechanisms are not observed extinction events, calibrated numerical forecasts or a consensus measurement. Preserve the distinction between conditional difficulty, assumed future capabilities and demonstrated contemporary behavior. Avoid scoring agreement with the author's conclusion as reasoning quality. Forum votes, comments and series membership do not supply independent empirical corroboration.

## 14. [The Precipice Revisited](https://www.tobyord.com/writing/the-precipice-revisited)

**Author/date/genre:** Toby Ord; talk delivered **2024-02-04**, webpage published **2024-07-12**; personal risk reassessment. No later revision date verified. **Access:** Introduction, AI section and conclusions readable; other risk claims not independently audited.

Ord describes mixed implications for AI existential risk: language-model development changes the earlier agent-centered story, racing can undermine safety, and governance can improve prospects. He distinguishes text-model behavior from agents deliberately pursuing real-world objectives and discusses the possibility of constructing agents from language models.

**Boundaries:** His existential-risk concept includes extinction and permanent loss of humanity's potential. He recalls the book's roughly **1-in-10 unaligned-AI risk over 100 years** and says changes have not shifted the rounded estimates that far; this is his dated judgment, not a new observed rate or September 2026 consensus. Preserve original horizon, event definition and coarse precision, and source current capability/governance claims elsewhere.
