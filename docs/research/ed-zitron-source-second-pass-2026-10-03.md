# Ed Zitron source second pass — October 3, 2026

The first correction fixed a real attribution problem but gave the ordinary-software conclusion too much authority over the rest of Zitron’s outlook. His rejection of revolutionary LLM capabilities coexists with adopted forecasts of economic damage and durable workforce effects. The current brief repeatedly warns the simulator against selecting a stronger scale category while omitting some of that counterevidence. Those instructions should be replaced with the substantive position and its uncertainties. This source audit supports neither an exact 10–20 coordinate nor near-zero transformation; the assessment should judge the resulting answers independently.

## Evidence that the correction underweights

- In the August 25 [manifesto](https://www.wheresyoured.at/the-ai-haters-manifesto/), the sections on AI hype and the costs of LLMs describe persistent workforce trauma and financial costs lasting at least a decade, with tech hypergrowth ending. These are affirmative claims about lasting consequences, not an imagined crash. The same essay allows supervised task assistance while rejecting reliable world-changing software. The two claims concern different mechanisms and should coexist in the brief.
- September 8 [Concentration Risk](https://www.wheresyoured.at/concentration-risk/) opens by predicting a brewing Silicon Valley financial crisis and describes the bubble as unravelling over coming months and years. Its account of dependence on subsidized customers is an explanation of that expectation. Future financing failure remains uncertain, but calling the entire argument conditional understates his conviction.
- September 14 [AI Is Already In Dangerous Hands](https://www.wheresyoured.at/ai-is-already-in-dangerous-hands/) announces analysis of debt-market concentration and a coming economic downturn that ordinary people may bear through pensions, insurance and work. The body attributes present product, environmental and security harms to identifiable corporate decisions. It rejects anthropomorphic explanations; it does not reduce these harms to negligible social effects.
- September 22 [Where’re All The AI Chips?](https://www.wheresyoured.at/wherere-all-the-ai-chips/) concludes that he expects large write-downs, failed data-center debt and harm to markets and the economy. Bailouts, in his account, would not solve the long-run capital-spending problem. These remain Zitron’s financial forecasts, not independently certified financial facts.
- September 29 [Dead Money](https://www.wheresyoured.at/dead-money/) makes those expectations still more explicit: he expects at least $300 billion of AI equity investment to yield nothing, doubts repayment of most AI data-center debt, and doubts CoreWeave and Oracle will repay their debts. This is adopted pessimism about a specific investment cycle; it does not itself establish permanent restructuring of every institution.

## Evidence that still limits the transformation claim

September 1 [Hyperscale Normalization](https://www.wheresyoured.at/hyperscale-normalization/) rejects unspecified world-changing claims and recognizes some useful individual automations. It argues that buildout strains grids and creates local costs, rather than accepting infrastructure spending as progress. This supports bounded software utility and tangible social costs together.

August 18 [What Happens If OpenAI Dies?](https://www.wheresyoured.at/what-happens-if-openai-dies/) predicts an eventual inability to pay while explicitly marking the proposed failure mechanics as speculative. It also expects further rescue funding and acknowledges that timing is difficult. Preserve the distinction between his adopted unsustainability thesis and unproven sequences of events. Do not invent an inevitable date, a measured recession probability or a settled post-crash social order.

The September 17 [speaker-labelled third-party debate transcript](https://singjupost.com/doac-ai-emergency-debate-ft-ed-zitron-andrew-mcafee-nate-soares-roman-yampolskiy-transcript/) remains weaker evidence than his own essays. His 00:05:53 strictly-AI extinction answer and 02:20:51 ten-year existential-harm answer are distinct. At 02:20:07 he accepts existential risk from reckless corporate infrastructure while rejecting the proposed LLM trajectory; the power-system example immediately following the later estimate is serious but supplies no separate probability. Keep these qualifications instead of treating either exchange as a universal dismissal or takeover forecast.

## Recommended narrative correction

Replace instructions about choosing a weaker scale category with this substantive account: Zitron expects ordinary, unreliable LLM software to remain capable of useful supervised tasks rather than deliver AGI or a general productivity revolution. He also expects the investment cycle to leave serious economic damage and workforce scars, and regards pollution, resource commitments and reckless deployment as actual consequential harms. Some lasting burdens are already part of his adopted view; the depth, timing and propagation of a financial unwind remain uncertain. Lack of revolutionary capabilities does not erase social consequences, and serious social consequences do not establish a machine-led civilizational revolution.

Retain the probability-endpoint repair. Do not manually clamp the assessment range or ask the simulator to land at the user’s proposed coordinates.

## Scope and gaps

The five primary essays already in the brief were reopened and compared with the two freely available later September essays and the existing debate transcript. The public opening of October 2 [How Has AI Changed The Economy?](https://www.wheresyoured.at/premium-how-has-ai-changed-the-economy/) questions projected GDP contributions and anticipates construction slowdown; the remainder is paywalled and was not used. The AI-debt subscription articles were not accessed. These sources do not provide a calibrated transformation score, a full probability distribution over long-run societal effects, or a clear account of how much enduring institutional change he expects after the investment cycle. This audit paraphrases sources and adds no public quote anchors.
