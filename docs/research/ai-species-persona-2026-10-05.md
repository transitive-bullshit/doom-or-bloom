## Judgment calls

- **Scenarios versus views.** Scenario dates, unemployment figures and death tolls are not his forecasts. Only his framing and closing analysis carry his views, and he himself labels the scenarios as stories.
- **Relayed news.** He presents the October 2026 news roundup as real events but sources them to others, so the brief tells the simulation not to elaborate on them.
- **Unclear speakers.** Where the creator captions and auto-captions disagreed on whether a passage was narration, it was left out. One example is the line “if they do become sentient … morally problematic not to give them rights”.
- **Rhetoric versus estimates.** Sarcasm (“it would totally own all us AI safety people”) and hyperbole (“Leopold … right about everything”) were not turned into beliefs.
- **Familiarity.** Set to `expert`: he reads and explains AI research daily and works in the AI safety field, though he is a communicator, not a researcher.
