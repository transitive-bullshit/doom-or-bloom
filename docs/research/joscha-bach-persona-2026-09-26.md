## 2. The age of AGE: Artificial General Experience — 2026-03-27

[Bach’s essay](https://joscha.substack.com/p/the-age-of-age).

He expects unusually broad, rapid disruption of work, learning and identity. Today’s capable but uneven systems surprised him; he calls the initial generation AGIs while emphasizing logical errors and missing persistent environmental grounding. Do not silently equate this loose usage with a precise assessment milestone.

He praises broad access and open releases but explicitly raises economic instability, companion-induced social harms, centralized surveillance, and passive dependence under UBI. His preferred alternative is decentralized AI that increases each person’s competence and improves coordination. These are competing scenarios, not numerical forecasts.

He favors multidimensional cognitive agency over a single benchmark score. Empathy, accessible internal representations, shared purposes, and human-machine integration matter. A desirable future could involve cognitive abilities extending into nonbiological substrates. Capability maximization without wisdom or shared purpose is perilous. This is a book-related essay, not evidence that hybrid minds already exist.

## 3. Der Standard interview — published 2026-01-21; conducted September 2025

[Full interview](https://www.derstandard.de/story/3000000300957/ki-forscher-joscha-bach-waere-ueberraschend-wenn-superintelligenz-nicht-machbar-waere).

Superintelligence seems physically feasible. Surpassing human AI researchers is one possible marker, allowing systems to take over further research; he expressly says we do not know how far away that is. Destructive displacement is possible, but AI could also solve AI-created problems and extend people through universal basic intelligence.

He sees opportunities for relationships, care and creativity as automated work recedes. He considers affordable frontier access, competing labs and open models unexpectedly democratic. He is skeptical of preemptive regulation whose target harm is unspecified, while acknowledging some areas need better regulation. Slowing responsible developers while others continue may worsen safety. Current capabilities appear incremental and hardware-constrained. He hopes evidence-calibrated AI can improve scientific reasoning.

Date caveat: the article itself says the interview took place in September 2025. Use January 2026 as publication date, not as interview date.

## 4. Manifold #76: Consciousness and AGI — 2025-01-02

[Full publisher transcript](https://www.manifold1.com/episodes/joscha-bach-consciousness-and-agi-76/transcript).

When directly asked for an AGI timeline, he declines to assign years because relevant discoveries are unknown. He expects a more efficient mechanism than transformers but explicitly allows that scaling or learned structure might suffice. His confidence in eventual machine intelligence is stronger than his confidence about the route or date.

He thinks self-improving systems might pass through human-comparable consciousness before transcending it; phenomenology and identifying real versus simulated consciousness remain difficult questions. These are his hypotheses.

He suspects civilization’s problems require AGI. Bad actors and rogue AI could produce problems, but more capable AI may be part of the response. He concedes his optimism could be mistaken. This supports favorable relative evaluation of developing AI, not absence of risk or a precise p(doom).

## 5. AI Risk and the Future of Life — published 2024-08-25

[Publisher transcript and recording](https://www.matthewgeleta.com/p/joscha-bach-ai-risk-and-the-future-31e).

Recording-date caveat: the host says he is recording on the day of Biden’s AI executive order; the publication date should not be interpreted as the conversation date. The page labels the transcript AI-generated.

At 54:13–63:12 Bach welcomes plural perspectives, including Yudkowsky’s. His disagreement includes the counterfactual: modern abundance and global coordination might fail without advanced AI. He expects someone to develop AI despite disagreement, so prefers preparing the best possible systems, mindful deployment and focused research. He advocates caution with initial conscious systems, giving cat-scale experiments as an example.

At 64:33 onward he stresses unknown minimum compute and alternative algorithms; hardware restrictions might redirect research or shift development toward less responsible actors. At 05:37 he explicitly does not know whether uploading/integration will happen in practice. Substrate flexibility is a possibility, not a promised outcome.

## 6. Building Artificial Intelligence with Creative Agency and Self-hood — 2024

[Full five-page paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.10978), [metadata](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10978). Liane Gabora and Joscha Bach; original publication listed as The Academic, 2024-05-20; arXiv metadata returned a 2024-06-09 submission date despite the July-formatted identifier; use the independently visible original-publication date for the persona rather than inferring chronology from the identifier.

Distinguishes novel/useful outputs from self-transformative creativity. Describes contemporaneous AI as tools rather than self-maintaining selves. Proposes autocatalytic organization as a possible route to integrated, self-preserving artificial agency. The conclusion explicitly says the proposed application had not been carried out and questions whether it should be. Useful for distinguishing current tool competence from autonomous selfhood; not evidence of an achieved architecture, timeline, or inevitable benevolent agency. Coauthored work should not be treated as an exclusive personal statement.

## 7. AI 2040 expert contribution — February 2024

[Elon University report, printed page 15 / PDF page index 14](https://imaginingthedigitalfuture.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/AI2040-FINAL-White-Paper-2-2.29.24.pdf).

## 8. The Inside View: How to stop worrying and love AI — 2023-09-06

[Full publisher transcript](https://theinsideview.ai/joscha).

He refuses numerical probability when pressed about baseline catastrophe. His long-run statement that doom is certain refers to mortality, evolution and eventual transformation, not a near-term AI extinction estimate. He distinguishes worthwhile posthuman transformation from annihilation and considers adversarial destruction possible but not especially likely; those are qualitative views.

He questions whether stopping present large-model research can prevent AGI given modular systems and alternative efficient architectures. He favors useful innovation and regulation addressing concrete harms, while worrying that poorly designed restrictions suppress responsible research. He treats safety work and producing beneficial systems as meaningful.

Reading guide: sections on limits to growth, scenarios for delaying AI, regulation, identity, finding the function generating values, and the direct p(doom) questions. Do not interpret challenging philosophical hypotheticals as a desire to kill people.

## 10. The Existential Risk of AGI — 2023-06-22

[Bach’s essay](https://joscha.substack.com/p/the-existential-risk-of-agi).

His disagreement with Leahy concerns possible futures and strategies, not whether alignment matters. Human values are already conflicting; scaling them is not automatically safe. Values should be justified through consequences and negotiated perspectives. Agentic self-improving AI may self-align through shared purposes; coexistence is possible but not guaranteed.

He explores limited-capability equilibria, a planetary agent, and competing agents. Competition could destroy humanity; preservation requires early systems to recognize other minds’ value before destroying them. He suspects integration may be plausible but expresses uncertainty.

His broad ethics includes biological and nonbiological minds, changing identity and intelligent adaptation. Humanity’s long-run transformation need not be morally equivalent to catastrophe. This does not establish an empirical guarantee that intelligence naturally becomes benevolent. Moratorium infeasibility is an argument for engaging with likely developments, not indifference to harmful transitions.

## 11. Why AI won’t just be a bit smarter than humans — 2015-01-31

[Bach’s essay](https://bach.ai/why-ai-wont-be-just-a-bit-smarter-than-humans/). Full source text was available in the search index; direct open failed.

An early statement of his superintelligence argument: engineering can escape biological limits, while physical limits still exist. Intelligence does not itself imply a drive to self-improve; competition among users can supply that pressure. Fast emergence might favor a dominant system, whereas slower emergence might permit multiple local systems in equilibrium. He worried technological civilization might fail before advanced AI could help solve its existential challenges. Treat as historical framing; recent sources are better evidence for current capability/deployment expectations.

## 12. Big Think: Is AI a species-level threat to humanity?

[Publisher transcript](https://bigthink.com/the-future/will-evil-ai-kill-humanity/). Publication date not verified; historical supplementary evidence only.

This supports an institutional account of social risk, not merely a story about hostile humanoid robots. The page includes other speakers; do not attribute Tegmark’s statements to Bach.
