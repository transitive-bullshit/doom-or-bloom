## Access and attribution boundaries

- Gebru’s February PDF exposes a month, not an exact day; its metadata retains `2026-02`. The August interview was inspected, but its source summary is deliberately minimal. The older paper was used at abstract scope. DAIR’s undated index is institutional corroboration, not proof that Gebru personally performed every listed project.
- Narayanan’s essays were read on the authors’ site, including the limitations of the August experiments and the September conclusion. The brief keeps that newer safety discussion rather than relying only on the better-known 2025 framework. Kapoor is credited on joint sources.
- Acemoglu’s latest transcript materially updates the persona: do not reuse an old economic estimate as an immutable position. The Brookings HTML summary was read; this note does not claim full-paper review. Interviewer propositions are separated from his replies.
- Zuckerberg’s letters were readable in full. Commercial promises and proposed control mechanisms remain his arguments, not verified results. The newer letter expands rather than erases the older qualification about open releases.
- Bender’s preprint introduction and relevant framing were inspected; the First Monday source was read at abstract scope. Only her own answers in the joint Spanish interview support the interview synthesis. The book site supplies a description, not full-book access; the [publisher-distributed Google Books record](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_AI_Con.html?id=HqEZEQAAQBAJ) confirms May 13, 2025 publication. A September 15, 2026 podcast listing was found, but no accessible substantive transcript was inspected, so no claims are manufactured from its recency.

## Distinctions to preserve in review

The three critical perspectives should remain distinct: Gebru’s community-controlled engineering, Bender’s linguistic and framing critique, and Acemoglu’s labor-market mechanisms. Narayanan’s slower diffusion expectations must coexist with concrete present safety concerns. Zuckerberg’s positive forecast must coexist with his argument that access and power distribution determine outcomes. These are narrative checks for fidelity, not placement requirements.
