## 1. source.required-074 — AI Economic Indicators

[Required Stanford hub](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/project/indicators/).

Dates: [Project launch](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/news/stanford-digital-economy-lab-launches-the-ai-economic-indicators-tracking-ais-impact-on-the-economy/) 2026-06-10; hub revision date unknown. The [Canaries dashboard](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/project/indicators/canaries-dashboard/) labels 2026-07-22. [June research note](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/app/uploads/2026/08/ai_economic_indicators_june_2026_update.pdf) was updated 2026-08-10. No September dashboard vintage was inspected.

Methods/limits: The hub combines employment, adoption, macroeconomic signals, and consumer-surplus measures with different samples and evidence windows. Its Canaries methodology uses a rolling five-year balanced ADP-firm sample and occupation-coded workers. This excludes firm entry/exit and payroll-provider changes; it represents neither the entire US workforce nor all ADP clients. Exposure quintiles weight occupations equally, so a quintile need not contain 20% of workers. Employment/exposure relationships are observational. GDP-B's July valuations are willingness-to-accept among users, not measured GDP. The hub credits ADP collaboration and support from Coefficient Giving, Schmidt Sciences, and Siegel Family Endowment.

Access scope: Hub descriptions/disclosures, launch announcement, Canaries methods/update labels, and research-note methods inspected. Raw chart downloads and every child dashboard's current methodology remain unaudited; hub freshness cannot authorize newer series-specific numerical claims.

## 2. source.required-075 — Canaries in the Coal Mine?

[Required Stanford publication](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publication/canaries-in-the-coal-mine-six-facts-about-the-recent-employment-effects-of-artificial-intelligence/).

Dates/version: Original August 2025; revised 2026-08-12. Read [August 2026 PDF](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/app/uploads/2026/08/Canaries_August2026.pdf), with evidence through June 2026.

Methods/limits: Balanced ADP-firm panels start in 2021, or 2018 for extended comparisons; the main panel covers 3.5–5 million employees/month. It restricts to full-time, positive-earnings workers under 70. Occupational exposure proxies, not randomized AI adoption, define comparisons. The 19% headline is a kept-pace employment shortfall for ages 22–25 in the top two exposure quintiles versus the bottom three; it differs from earlier controlled-regression estimates and does not measure unemployment. Divergence primarily reflects hiring, with pre-existing trends, education attenuation, and stronger patterns in ADP than national surveys. Outcomes include headcounts, hiring/separations, and base compensation, which excludes bonuses and other pay components. Authors have Stanford affiliations. DEL funding and ADP support/data access are disclosed; ADP can review confidential information. Payroll records are proprietary.

Access scope: Targeted full-text data/methods, headline construction, hiring, compensation limitations, and funding/data-availability sections inspected. Authorable as descriptive evidence; AI-causal attribution and future displacement forecasts remain unsupported.

## 3. source.required-077 — Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier

[Required HBS 24-013 PDF](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/download.aspx?name=24-013.pdf).

Access/version: Exact retrieval failed once; indexed 2023-09-22 copy remains partially read. Related [journal PDF](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/orsc.2025.21838) was published 2026-03-11, revised 2026-01-23. It dates the initial working-paper release to 2023-09-18; version identity is not byte equivalence.

Methods/limits: A preregistered experiment randomized 758 BCG consultants into two task arms, then no AI, GPT-4, or GPT-4 plus prompting overview, after baseline tasks. The tool used end-April 2023 GPT-4; exact participant collection dates were not established. Outcomes were completion, time, graded quality, and case correctness. Assigned AI improved inside-frontier performance; the discussion reports a 19-percentage-point correctness reduction for the single outside-frontier case. Subjective grading, selected consultants/tasks, incentives prioritizing timely quality, and one outside task limit transfer. This establishes experimental treatment effects, not 2026 model boundaries or economy-wide employment effects. Journal disclosures include BCG-affiliated coauthors and HBS institute/research funding.

Access scope: Related journal methods, outcomes, limitations, affiliations/funding, and release history inspected. Exact 2023 methods remain blocked; its previously indexed advisory disclosure remains separately attributed. Do not silently replace historical estimates with journal results.
