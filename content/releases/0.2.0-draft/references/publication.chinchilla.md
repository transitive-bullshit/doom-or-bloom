## Neutral context

A study of balancing training data and model size under a compute budget.

## Reported facts

Hoffmann and colleagues trained hundreds of models to study compute-optimal scaling. Their Chinchilla demonstration used a smaller model and more training data than Gopher at the same compute budget, improving reported evaluations.

## Interpretations

The work revises engineering allocations, rather than settling all capability or safety forecasts.

## Known unknowns

Optimal allocations depend on assumptions, compute accounting, data quality, and the target task.

## Claim support

Supports the importance of jointly scaling model size and training data. Does not show that parameter count alone determines intelligence or that benchmark progress removes deployment risks.
