## Neutral context

A workplace study of conversational assistance and support productivity.

## Reported facts

Brynjolfsson, Li, and Raymond study a staggered conversational-assistant rollout among 5,179 customer-support agents. The working-paper abstract reports 14% more resolved issues per hour on average, with larger gains for novices and lower-skilled workers and minimal gains for experienced workers. It also reports improved customer sentiment and retention.

## Interpretations

Gains and distributional effects depend on workers and workplace conditions.

## Known unknowns

Workplace-specific results and heterogeneous effects do not establish economy-wide employment outcomes. A later journal version may differ.

## Claim support

Supports measured productivity gains in the studied support setting; does not show all workers benefit equally or prove net job creation.
