## Neutral context

Genre: Philosophical essay and coordination-failure metaphor. Scott Alexander personifies destructive competitive incentives as Moloch.

## Reported facts

Selected sections connect local incentive traps with collectively unwanted outcomes and loss of valued goals; later passages consider hypothetical AI futures.

## Interpretations

Moloch is a metaphor, not a literal agent. The essay offers a lens on coordination rather than a measured causal law.

## Known unknowns

Examples do not establish that competition always causes catastrophe or that one coordination scheme solves it. Reader comments are separate authorship.

## Claim support

Supports discussing frontier races, institutional incentives and coordination assumptions. Does not measure current capabilities or catastrophe probability.

## Related entries

The unilateralist paper analyzes a narrower decision structure; the metaphor is not independent empirical corroboration.
