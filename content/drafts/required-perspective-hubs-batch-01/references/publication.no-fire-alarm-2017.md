## Neutral context

Genre: Historical epistemic/preparedness essay by Eliezer Yudkowsky, published by MIRI. Introduction and selected forecasting arguments were inspected.

## Reported facts

He argues people may wait for socially recognized permission to respond while breakthroughs may lack timely obvious warnings. He criticizes forecasts based solely on present difficulty or ignorance of solutions and favors concrete preparation.

## Interpretations

This is an uncertainty/collective-response argument, not proof that AGI is imminent or no warning signal can be useful.

## Known unknowns

Historical analogies and social-psychology citations were not independently audited. Not every passage was inspected.

## Claim support

Supports attributing preparedness concerns and scrutinizing forecasting premises. Does not establish a current incident, timeline or numerical catastrophe belief.

## Related entries

AGI Ruin discusses conditional practical difficulty in a later essay; thematic agreement is not empirical corroboration.
