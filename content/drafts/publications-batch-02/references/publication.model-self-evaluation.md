## Neutral context

A study of elicited model judgments about answer correctness.

## Reported facts

Kadavath and colleagues investigate model judgments of answer correctness and whether a question is answerable. They report encouraging calibration under suitable formats and improved self-evaluation when multiple samples are considered. Predicting whether the model knows an answer partially transfers across tasks, with calibration difficulties on new tasks.

## Interpretations

Correctness signals depend on how they are elicited and where they transfer.

## Known unknowns

Confidence depends on elicitation and task distribution; transfer is imperfect. Self-evaluation is not demonstrated consciousness.

## Claim support

Supports usable but conditional correctness signals. Does not guarantee calibrated probabilities for Jev or every new application without task-specific validation.
