## Neutral context

A task-exposure analysis of possible labor-market effects.

## Reported facts

Eloundou and colleagues assess US occupational task exposure using human judgments and GPT-4 classifications. They estimate that roughly 80% of workers could have at least 10% of tasks affected, and about 19% could have at least half affected. Exposure rises when considering complementary software. They explicitly avoid adoption-timeline predictions.

## Interpretations

Task exposure and realized employment outcomes are different quantities.

## Known unknowns

Potential exposure is not realized adoption, displacement, unemployment, or measured productivity growth.

## Claim support

Supports broad task-level exposure under the authors' rubric. Does not predict that 80% of jobs disappear or specify when economic transformation occurs.
